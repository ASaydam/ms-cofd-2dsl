# Decision log

Every choice that shapes the dataset, with its date and reason. The numbers behind the early decisions come from a
preliminary study: 30 obstacle geometries (the tallest and a median obstacle of every family and size) solved to
steady state on three meshes (1, 0.5 and 0.25 mm) with first- and second-order upwind convection.

| Date | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| 2026-10-01 | Every run converges to initial residuals of 1e-6 (residualControl); endTime is only a cap | A loose criterion (U 1e-3) stops fine-mesh runs after a few hundred iterations, far from steady state; the coarse-to-fine differences are then mostly solver error, not discretization error |
| 2026-10-01 | Three resolutions for every case: 1 mm, 0.5 mm (4x), 0.25 mm (16x) | A mesh convergence study for every case (order, Richardson extrapolation, grid convergence index): the uncertainty of every 16x label. The 0.5 mm run costs ~8 % of the total |
| 2026-10-04 | Second-order upwind convection (linearUpwind) for all three meshes | Preliminary study: with first-order upwind the order of convergence is ~1 and the scheme sets the error; with linearUpwind the 1 mm error of the recirculation length drops from 16 % to 1.9 % and the 0.25 mm error to 0.3 %, at slightly lower cost (0.25 mm: 3.5 instead of 4.3 core-hours per case) |
| 2026-10-04 | A first-order run on the 1 mm mesh as well (base_fo) | ~2 core-minutes per case; a cheaper, more diffusive low-fidelity solution next to the second-order one |
| 2026-10-04 | 4500 geometries (300 per size class and family), all generated from one seed | Fully reproducible from the code and the seed |
| 2026-10-04 | One size scheme for all size classes: nominal size × scale (normal 1, smaller 0.65, larger 1.35) × (1 ± 10 % width, 1 ± 20 % height) | Size classes differ only in scale. Nominal sizes: rectangle and diamond 50 × 25 mm, triangles 42.5 × 42.5 mm, circle 25 × 25 mm |
| 2026-10-04 | Rotation ±45° about the centroid; shift −30…+10 mm along and ±15 mm across the channel | Variety of orientation and position. ±45° keeps every family's identity (a right triangle still points downstream); the shifts are biased upstream to leave room for the wake |
| 2026-10-04 | Minimum gaps: 5 mm to the walls, 60 mm to the inlet, 150 mm to the outlet; invalid obstacles are drawn again | Valid meshes and room for the wake. Rejection is rare (at most 8.3 % of draws, for larger rectangles and right triangles), so the distributions barely change |
| 2026-10-04 | Channel 360 × 120 mm, obstacle nominally 130 mm from the inlet | In a 260 mm channel the reverse flow behind large obstacles (blockage ≥ 0.49) reached the outlet in 6 of 30 cases of the preliminary study; 100 mm more downstream, ~38 % more cells |
| 2026-10-04 | Fixed inlet velocity 0.1 m/s | Steady flow for every case (Reynolds number up to ~90 by obstacle height); varying it risks unsteady cases |
| 2026-10-04 | Fixed, seeded splits (train / val / test) and out-of-distribution test sets from the tails of rotation, position and blockage | Comparable benchmark results |
| 2026-10-04 | Locally refined 1 mm meshes for the test and out-of-distribution cases | The baseline a learned coarse-to-fine correction has to beat: spending the compute on the solver near the obstacle instead |
| 2026-10-04 | Container: OpenFOAM v2512 (ESI) and a micromamba environment (gmsh 4.15.2, VTK 9.7.0, Python 3.14), built by GitHub Actions, run with Apptainer | Current software on a cluster with an old operating system (CentOS 7); one image anyone can run. The image is public on ghcr.io |
| 2026-10-04 | VTK (vtkOpenFOAMReader + vtkResampleToImage) for the arrays | The filters behind ParaView's OpenFOAM reader and Resample To Image, headless and without a GUI build |
| 2026-10-04 | Meshes are made inside each task; every task archives its own output and moves it off the cluster | The cluster quota is 1 TiB and must never be exceeded; the raw output is ~3.4 TB. Submissions pause above 600 GiB used (quota usage, see the mirroring row) or 50 GiB not uploaded |
| 2026-10-04 | Archives keep everything (case folder with both kept time steps, mesh, logs, attribute work folder) | Any attribute can be recomputed later without running the solver again |
| 2026-10-04 | One SLURM job per task (one run of one case), at most 280 queued | Per-task timing and memory for the cost study, single tasks can be rerun; the queue never floods the scheduler |
| 2026-10-04 | Code reaches the cluster as a git archive of a committed revision (REVISION file) | No credentials on the cluster; every result records the exact commit |
| 2026-10-04 | Pressure drop also on cross-sections 10 mm from the inlet and the outlet (dp_sections) | The area average on the inlet patch includes the singular inlet corners and does not converge with the mesh |
| 2026-10-04 | New attributes: mean wall shear (area-averaged \|τ_w\| on the obstacle), mean vorticity (area-averaged \|ω\|, first 5 mm left out), lift with its pressure and friction parts in the mesh study | Requested for the study. Preliminary study (30 geometries, second order): they converge (mean wall shear order 1.9, 1 mm error 2.3 %; mean vorticity order 1.1, 0.5 %); lift is ~9 % of the drag without rotation, its 1 mm error is 9 % of the lift itself |
| 2026-10-04 | A fourth, coarser level: 2 mm, second order (0.25x, arrays 180 × 60) | With second-order convection the 1 mm mesh is within 1–3 % of the mesh-independent value for almost every integral attribute, so a meaningful coarse-to-fine task needs a coarser input. Costs ~0.01 core-hours per case |
| 2026-10-04 | Benchmark targets: all mesh-converging attributes with their uncertainty, surface distributions; peak values included but flagged | Peak wall shear and peak vorticity grow with refinement at sharp corners (singular), they are not mesh-independent |
| 2026-10-04 | Attribute calculations checked across OpenFOAM versions | On identical flow fields, the attributes computed with v2512 equal those of v1706 for all 30 × 3 cases of the preliminary study |
| 2026-10-04 | Recirculation length and area count only the reverse flow connected to the obstacle (connected over the mesh faces) | Preliminary study: in 4 of 30 cases a thin, slow reverse-flow layer along a channel wall (0.1–2.6 mm from the wall, \|u_x\| ≤ 10⁻³ m/s) reached the outlet and set the recirculation length to the distance to the outlet, on some meshes only (74.5, 74.0, 101.5 mm on 1, 0.5, 0.25 mm). Connected to the obstacle: 74.5, 74.0, 74.5 mm; unchanged in the other 26 cases |
| 2026-10-04 | New attribute outlet_reverse_flow: reverse flow rate in the last 5 mm of the channel relative to the net flow rate | Flags the cases in which the outlet condition sets part of the solution. Preliminary study (260 mm channel): 0.22 for the highest-blockage case, 4·10⁻⁴ to 8·10⁻³ for three more |
| 2026-10-04 | Footprint limits in quota units: the cluster's /scratch is BeeGFS with buddy mirroring, the quota usage counts every file twice | Measured: ~445 GiB of data in the user's folders, ~885 GiB quota usage; files report the stripe pattern Buddy Mirror. The 1 TiB quota is ~512 GiB of data; the upload backlog limit (file sizes) is lowered to 50 GiB |
| 2026-10-05 | A mesh that fails checkMesh is made again with gmsh option variants (more smoothing, then simple recombination); the variant used is recorded per run | Pilot: 1 of 45 0.25 mm meshes had two overlapping triangles (a fold left by the quad recombination), and the solver diverged on it. With Mesh.Smoothing = 5 the same geometry gives a valid all-quad mesh. Changing the settings only for the failing meshes keeps all other meshes as they are |
| 2026-10-05 | Blockage ratio (obstacle height / channel height) at most 0.45; an obstacle above it is drawn again. The inlet velocity stays 0.1 m/s | Pilot (45 geometries at the extremes of blockage, rotation and position, 360 x 120 mm channel): on the 1 mm mesh with second-order convection, all 30 obstacles below a blockage of 0.47 converged; of the 15 from 0.47 to 0.76, 8 did not (initial residuals stuck at 1e-3 to 1e-2 after 10,000 iterations), others needed 5,000 to 6,500 iterations. The Reynolds number with the mean velocity in the gaps and the obstacle height is 105 to 230 there, where the flow past a confined obstacle becomes unsteady: no steady solution. The same obstacles caused the reverse flow at the outlet. Effect: 56 normal obstacles and about half of the larger rectangles and triangles are drawn again (11.8 % of all draws); larger rectangles are mostly little rotated (mean rotation 12.7 deg instead of 22.5 deg), since a large rectangle turned by 45 deg is taller than the limit |
| 2026-10-05 | Scale of the larger size class 1.3 instead of 1.35 | Fewer larger obstacles above the blockage limit |
| 2026-10-05 | Blockage limit 0.40 instead of 0.45 | Second pilot (24 obstacles at the 0.45 limit, the larger class at 1.3x): on the 1 mm mesh with second-order convection 6 of them did not converge (residuals stuck at 4e-3 to 6e-3), all with a blockage of 0.448 to 0.450; the onset depends on the shape (3 of 4 left triangles at the limit, 2 of 7 right triangles, the circle; rectangles converge even higher). In both pilots every obstacle with a blockage up to 0.41 converged (31). The 2 mm mesh is less robust: a run that does not converge there is flagged, the case is kept. Effect: 20.4 % of the cases are drawn again at least once; larger rectangles mean rotation 10 deg (1 % above 30 deg), larger triangles mean height 50 mm instead of 55 mm and 21 % above 30 deg; smaller unchanged |
| 2026-10-05 | A case whose 1 mm second-order run (base_lu) stops at its iteration cap is flagged as without a steady solution: its 0.5 and 0.25 mm runs are not made. Classes with such cases are filled up to 300 with reserve geometries (indices 301 on, drawn from the same seed) at the end of the production | Third pilot (30 obstacles at the 0.40 limit): all converged on the 1 mm mesh except 4 short, blunt left triangles (normal size, blockage 0.397 to 0.400); the larger left triangles at the limit converge. In the dataset about 10 to 20 cases (~0.3 %) are expected. A lower limit only for left triangles would cut that family further on little data |
| 2026-10-05 | Change of the row above: a case without a steady solution is dropped with all its runs (also 2 mm and 1 mm first order, which therefore start only after base_lu converged) and replaced by the next of 30 reserve geometries of its size and family (indices 301 to 330, same seed, `python main.py geometry --reserve`) | A case belongs to the dataset with every level or not at all. First production day: 24 of 4500 cases (18 normal left triangles, 3 larger left triangles, 3 normal right triangles, blockage 0.36 to 0.40) |
| 2026-10-05 | Runs that stopped at the cap with residuals still falling (largest initial residual below 1e-4: 6 of the 24) get a cap of 25,000 iterations (`iterations.txt` in the data root) before their case is dropped | The convergence criterion stays 1e-6; the cap only ends a run. One of them stopped at 1.0e-6 |
| 2026-10-05 | Run order of the production (run_priority in config/slurm.yaml): base_lu, then the 2 mm and the 1 mm first-order runs of all cases, then 0.25 mm and 0.5 mm | Their convergence rates decide whether a case whose 2 mm or first-order run does not converge is dropped as well; both are cheap (~5 h of the allocation together) |
| 2026-10-05 | Time and memory per run from the pilots: 2 mm and 1 mm 2 h / 1 GB, 0.5 mm 3 h / 2 GB, 0.25 mm 12 h / 4 GB; at most 240 jobs queued | Solver peak memory 45 MB, 90 MB, 264 MB, 942 MB; longest converged run 0.1 h, 0.6 h (at the cap), 0.7 h, 4.7 h; meshing and resampling add memory. Interrupted runs continue where they stopped. The account allows 150 running and 300 queued jobs, shared with the validation runs |
| 2026-10-05 | Time limit of the 0.25 mm jobs 12 h → 16 h (jobs submitted from then on) | Pilot 1 on the 360 mm domain (26 cases with blockage ≤ 0.40): solver time 2.4 to 15.4 h, mean 7.3 h, rising with the blockage ratio (≈ −2.2 + 34.6 × blockage h, r = 0.77). With 16 h nearly every run ends in one job instead of waiting in the queue again; a longer run still continues in a second job |
| 2026-10-06 | 2 mm runs that stopped at the 10,000-iteration cap (37 of 4500; every 1 mm first-order run converged) are run again. The 10 whose residual still fell (trend of the last 2000 iterations reaching 1e-6 by ~43,000 iterations) get a cap of 50,000 and otherwise the same settings. The 27 with a flat residual (1e-3 to 3e-2) start from their converged 1 mm solution (mapFields, `init_from base_lu`) with SIMPLE and under-relaxation p 0.3 / U 0.7 (template `second-order-relaxed`, which differs from `second-order` only in system/fvSolution), cap 25,000 (`case_settings.txt`, `iterations.txt`). The first runs are kept (results/0.25x/superseded, archives/0.25x/superseded on Drive) | All 37 have blockage 0.353 to 0.399 (35 triangles, 2 larger circles); their 1 mm second-order runs converged, after 5,700 to 16,600 iterations. The converged solution depends only on the mesh and the discretisation, not on the initial fields or the solver settings, so a stall of the SIMPLEC iteration is not a property of the steady solution |
| 2026-10-06 | The 0.25 mm runs of these 37 cases are on hold (`hold.txt`) until the end of the production; then it is decided whether cases with a 2 mm run that does not converge are kept with a flag or dropped and replaced | Dropping would remove the cases with the highest blockage, 12 of them from the 224 cases of the blockage out-of-distribution set, and replace them with random, mostly lower-blockage draws; holding saves ~400 core-hours until then |
