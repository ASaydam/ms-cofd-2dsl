# Decision log

Every choice that shapes the dataset, with its date and reason. The numbers behind the early decisions come from a
preliminary study: 30 obstacle geometries (the tallest and a median obstacle of every family and size) solved to
steady state on three meshes (1, 0.5 and 0.25 mm) with first- and second-order upwind convection.

| Date | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| 2026-10-01 | Every run converges to initial residuals of 1e-6 (residualControl); endTime is only a cap | A loose criterion (U 1e-3) stops fine-mesh runs after a few hundred iterations, far from steady state; the coarse-to-fine differences are then mostly solver error, not discretization error |
| 2026-10-01 | Three resolutions for every case: 1 mm, 0.5 mm (4x), 0.25 mm (16x) | A mesh convergence study for every case (order, Richardson extrapolation, grid convergence index): the uncertainty of every 16x label. The 0.5 mm run costs ~8 % of the total |
| 2026-10-04 | Second-order upwind convection (linearUpwind) for all three meshes | Preliminary study: with first-order upwind the order of convergence is ~1 and the scheme sets the error; with linearUpwind the 1 mm error of the recirculation length drops from 16 % to 1.9 % and the 0.25 mm error to 0.3 %, at slightly lower cost (0.25 mm: 3.5 instead of 4.3 core-hours per case) |
| 2026-10-04 | A first-order run on the 1 mm mesh as well (base_fo) | ~2 core-minutes per case; a cheaper, more diffusive low-fidelity solution next to the second-order one |
| 2026-10-04 | 4500 geometries (300 per size class and family), all generated from one seed | Fully reproducible from the code and the seed |
| 2026-10-04 | One size scheme for all size classes: nominal size × scale (normal 1, smaller 0.65, larger 1.35) × (1 ± 10 % width, 1 ± 20 % height) | Size classes differ only in scale. Nominal sizes: rectangle and diamond 50 × 25 mm, triangles 42.5 × 42.5 mm, circle 25 × 25 mm |
| 2026-10-04 | Rotation ±45° about the centroid; shift −30…+10 mm along and ±15 mm across the channel | Variety of orientation and position. ±45° keeps every family's identity (a right triangle still points downstream); the shifts are biased upstream to leave room for the wake |
| 2026-10-04 | Minimum gaps: 5 mm to the walls, 60 mm to the inlet, 150 mm to the outlet; invalid obstacles are drawn again | Valid meshes and room for the wake. Rejection is rare (at most 8.3 % of draws, for larger rectangles and right triangles), so the distributions barely change |
| 2026-10-04 | Channel 360 × 120 mm, obstacle nominally 130 mm from the inlet | In a 260 mm channel the reverse flow behind large obstacles (blockage ≥ 0.49) reached the outlet in 6 of 30 cases of the preliminary study; 100 mm more downstream, ~38 % more cells |
| 2026-10-04 | Fixed inlet velocity 0.1 m/s | Steady flow for every case (Reynolds number up to ~90 by obstacle height); varying it risks unsteady cases |
| 2026-10-04 | Fixed, seeded splits (train / val / test) and out-of-distribution test sets from the tails of rotation, position and blockage | Comparable benchmark results |
| 2026-10-04 | Locally refined 1 mm meshes for the test and out-of-distribution cases | The baseline a learned coarse-to-fine correction has to beat: spending the compute on the solver near the obstacle instead |
| 2026-10-04 | Container: OpenFOAM v2512 (ESI) and a micromamba environment (gmsh 4.15.2, VTK 9.7.0, Python 3.14), built by GitHub Actions, run with Apptainer | Current software on a cluster with an old operating system (CentOS 7); one image anyone can run. The image is public on ghcr.io |
| 2026-10-04 | VTK (vtkOpenFOAMReader + vtkResampleToImage) for the arrays | The filters behind ParaView's OpenFOAM reader and Resample To Image, headless and without a GUI build |
| 2026-10-04 | Meshes are made inside each task; every task archives its own output and moves it off the cluster | The cluster quota is 1 TiB and must never be exceeded; the raw output is ~3.4 TB. Submissions pause above 600 GiB used or 200 GiB not uploaded |
| 2026-10-04 | Archives keep everything (case folder with both kept time steps, mesh, logs, attribute work folder) | Any attribute can be recomputed later without running the solver again |
| 2026-10-04 | One SLURM job per task (one run of one case), at most 280 queued | Per-task timing and memory for the cost study, single tasks can be rerun; the queue never floods the scheduler |
| 2026-10-04 | Code reaches the cluster as a git archive of a committed revision (REVISION file) | No credentials on the cluster; every result records the exact commit |
| 2026-10-04 | Pressure drop also on cross-sections 10 mm from the inlet and the outlet (dp_sections) | The area average on the inlet patch includes the singular inlet corners and does not converge with the mesh |
| 2026-10-04 | New attributes: mean wall shear (area-averaged \|τ_w\| on the obstacle), mean vorticity (area-averaged \|ω\|, first 5 mm left out), lift with its pressure and friction parts in the mesh study | Requested for the study. Preliminary study (30 geometries, second order): they converge (mean wall shear order 1.9, 1 mm error 2.3 %; mean vorticity order 1.1, 0.5 %); lift is ~9 % of the drag without rotation, its 1 mm error is 9 % of the lift itself |
| 2026-10-04 | A fourth, coarser level: 2 mm, second order (0.25x, arrays 180 × 60) | With second-order convection the 1 mm mesh is within 1–3 % of the mesh-independent value for almost every integral attribute, so a meaningful coarse-to-fine task needs a coarser input. Costs ~0.01 core-hours per case |
| 2026-10-04 | Benchmark targets: all mesh-converging attributes with their uncertainty, surface distributions; peak values included but flagged | Peak wall shear and peak vorticity grow with refinement at sharp corners (singular), they are not mesh-independent |
| 2026-10-04 | Attribute calculations checked across OpenFOAM versions | On identical flow fields, the attributes computed with v2512 equal those of v1706 for all 30 × 3 cases of the preliminary study |
| 2026-10-04 | Recirculation length and area count only the reverse flow connected to the obstacle (connected over the mesh faces) | Preliminary study: in 4 of 30 cases a thin, slow reverse-flow layer along a channel wall (0.1–2.6 mm from the wall, \|u_x\| ≤ 10⁻³ m/s) reached the outlet and set the recirculation length to the distance to the outlet, on some meshes only (74.5, 74.0, 101.5 mm on 1, 0.5, 0.25 mm). Connected to the obstacle: 74.5, 74.0, 74.5 mm; unchanged in the other 26 cases |
| 2026-10-04 | New attribute outlet_reverse_flow: reverse flow rate in the last 5 mm of the channel relative to the net flow rate | Flags the cases in which the outlet condition sets part of the solution. Preliminary study (260 mm channel): 0.22 for the highest-blockage case, 4·10⁻⁴ to 8·10⁻³ for three more |
